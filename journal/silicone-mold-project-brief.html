      MATERIAL GUIDES / PROJECT PLANNING

      Before making a silicone mold: five details to settle

      A good mold begins with a clear master, a release plan and the right silicone system. Settle the design questions before measuring a batch.

      By the ArFlair editorial team · 2 min read

      IN THIS GUIDE

      01   Inspect the master02   Choose the mold structure03   Test compatibility before the main pour04   Follow one documented system← All stories

      Before you begin

      • A clean master with a defined finished surface
      • A compatible silicone system and its TDS/SDS
      • A suitable mold box
      • Any sealer or release agent required by that system
      01

      Inspect the master

      Check the surface, fine details and undercuts. Every imperfection can transfer into the mold. Decide which faces need the best finish and how the finished casting will be removed without stressing thin features.

      02

      Choose the mold structure

      A simple open shape may suit a one-part mold, while more complex geometry can need a multi-part approach. Plan the opening, support and alignment before choosing the box dimensions. Confirm wall thickness with the silicone supplier.

      03

      Test compatibility before the main pour

      Some master materials, coatings and contaminants can interfere with specific silicone curing systems. Run the supplier’s recommended small compatibility test. Use a sealer or release only when it is suitable for the chosen materials.

      04

      Follow one documented system

      Use the silicone’s exact ratio, measuring basis, mixing method and cure schedule. Check whether degassing is recommended for that formulation. After curing, inspect the mold and trial it with the intended casting material before repeating production.

      TAKE THIS WITH YOU

      The master, silicone and casting material need to work together as one system.

      For material processing, use the current TDS and SDS for the exact product you select. Mixing ratios, cure conditions and suitable applications vary by formulation.
