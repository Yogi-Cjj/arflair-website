      FOR BRANDS

      Silicone mold sample inspection: detail, release and repeatability

      Approve a mold by casting the product you intend to sell. A useful sample review checks dimensions, detail, release and the finished result with the actual casting material.

      By the ArFlair editorial team · 4 min read · November 24, 2026

      IN THIS GUIDE

      01 Define what the dimensions describe02 Inspect the details that matter to your design03 Test with the intended casting material04 Review demolding and repeat runs05 Approve the product and packaging together← All stories

      Prepare for the review

      • Mold sample and agreed drawing or master dimensions
      • The intended resin or casting material and its instructions
      • An inspection sheet, photographs and approved finished sample
      01

      Define what the dimensions describe

      Distinguish overall mold size from cavity dimensions and finished casting dimensions. For a multi-cavity sheet, record the cavity count and compare each shape. Ask the supplier to clarify the measurement reference and any agreed tolerance before purchase. Do not infer finished-piece size from a photograph or assume a listed sheet measurement describes every cavity.

      02

      Inspect the details that matter to your design

      Look at the features the customer will notice: skull teeth, animal toes, bookmark edges or hanging-hole posts. Inspect the sample for visible damage and unwanted surface defects. Photograph the mold and then the fully set casting under consistent lighting. Agree on an acceptable reference sample rather than using an undefined request for perfect quality.

      03

      Test with the intended casting material

      Follow the casting material’s ratio, layer limit and curing or setting instructions. Resin, plaster and wax are different processes; suitability for one does not prove suitability for another. If candles are planned, confirm the mold’s compatibility with the specific wax process before ordering. For UV resin, include light access and the lamp setup in the review.

      04

      Review demolding and repeat runs

      Wait until the casting meets its handling instructions before release. Observe whether fine details survive demolding and whether the finished piece needs acceptable finishing work. Repeat the intended process on samples and record the result. Do not turn a short sample trial into an unsupported universal mold-lifetime claim; discuss expected use and care for the selected design.

      05

      Approve the product and packaging together

      List the mold configuration, included hardware, label, protective packaging, quantities and destination in the purchase brief. For a keychain kit, check both mold and hardware contents; for ornaments, review hanging-hole placement and any separately sourced cord. Keep the approved sample and a written checklist so incoming production can be compared with the same criteria.

      TAKE THIS WITH YOU

      A sample approval should identify the exact mold, casting process and acceptance criteria for the finished piece.

      Use the current instructions and safety documentation for your exact product. Technical figures quoted here refer to the named supplied TDS and its test conditions; they do not establish suitability for every project.
