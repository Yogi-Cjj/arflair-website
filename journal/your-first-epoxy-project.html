      TUTORIALS / BEGINNER

      Your first epoxy project: start small, make it yours

      A small coaster is a useful place to explore mixing, color and finishing before moving on to a larger idea. Here is how to plan a first sample with fewer unknowns.

      By the ArFlair editorial team · 2 min read

      IN THIS GUIDE

      01   Choose the project before the resin02   Prepare before you mix03   Measure accurately, then mix thoroughly04   Cast, protect and review← All stories

      Before you begin

      • An epoxy system suited to small castings
      • A clean, compatible casting form
      • Measuring cups, stir sticks and a level work surface
      • The protection specified in the product SDS
      01

      Choose the project before the resin

      Sketch the finished shape and measure its dimensions. A shallow decorative coaster and a thick block do not place the same demands on a resin. Check the selected product’s technical data sheet for its intended use, mixing method, batch limit and permitted casting depth.

      02

      Prepare before you mix

      Protect the work surface and arrange every tool within reach. Keep the casting form clean and dry, and check that it sits level. Read the SDS and use the specified gloves, eye protection and ventilation. Keep the working area separate from food preparation.

      03

      Measure accurately, then mix thoroughly

      Follow the exact ratio on your own product, including whether it is stated by weight or by volume. These are not interchangeable. Mix using the manufacturer’s method, reaching the sides and base of the cup. Work within the documented working time rather than trying to rescue a thickening batch.

      04

      Cast, protect and review

      Pour within the stated depth limit and cover the piece against dust without touching the surface. Follow the full curing schedule before demolding and finishing. Record your room conditions, batch size and result so your next sample starts from something you can repeat.

      TAKE THIS WITH YOU

      A successful small sample is a better starting point than a large, complicated first pour.

      For material processing, use the current TDS and SDS for the exact product you select. Mixing ratios, cure conditions and suitable applications vary by formulation.
